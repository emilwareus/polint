<threat_model>
Security enforcement: ASVS L1, block-high.

Assets:
- the sealed statement that a provider succeeded and its output identity is
  reusable;
- the provider closure used to certify a hard capability;
- the rule-dispatch decision preventing partial facts from reaching policy
  code;
- deterministic cold/warm policy answers.

Trust boundaries:
- cached payload bytes are untrusted until existing typed decode and payload
  validation succeed;
- provider execution is provisional until typed completion and authoritative
  global validation;
- diagnostic text and cache telemetry are observation channels, never trust
  inputs;
- semantic-store maintenance occurs outside the provider-trust boundary.

Abuse cases and controls:
- **Forged success (HIGH):** missing digest, warning text, empty facts, or a
  failed replacement is labeled succeeded. Control: closed tracker transitions,
  typed failure ledger, success-only identity invariant, and seal completeness.
- **Stale/invalid cache trust (HIGH):** a hit counter certifies malformed or
  stale output. Control: typed layer decode/validation before attempt success,
  invalid-evict/recompute proof, and semantic/telemetry separation.
- **Incomplete provider closure (HIGH):** a consumer or rule runs after a hard
  producer is failed, blocked, setup-missing, unsupported, or planned absent.
  Control: explicit audited current edges, plan-first gating, validation
  downgrades followed by deterministic fixed-point dependency closure, exact
  sorted direct blockers at each affected consumer, and production-runner
  pre-RuleCtx dispatch skip.
- **Unowned validation escape (HIGH):** a global authoritative issue cannot be
  attributed and some provider remains certified. Control: fail every
  provisional success for any unowned global issue.
- **Scope/public leakage (MEDIUM):** private trust vocabulary becomes a public
  enum/JSON/CLI contract. Control: crate-private types plus negative public
  surface scanning and unchanged prelude allowlist.

ASVS mapping:
- V1 Architecture: one explicit trust-sealing boundary, fail-closed dependency
  and validation transitions, least public visibility.
- V5 Validation: typed cache decode, structured validation ownership, closed
  state labels, bounded deterministic evidence.
- V7 Error handling: controlled failures remain typed and diagnostics do not
  become semantic state.

Any reproducible path that can forge `Succeeded`, execute a hard-dependent rule
after non-success closure, or leave provisional success after unowned
authoritative failure is HIGH and blocks completion. Do not absorb general Go
process/toolchain hardening; mark Go output non-success/non-reusable and stop if
that prerequisite is necessary.
</threat_model>
